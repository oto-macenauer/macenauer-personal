# House style

The voice of a working tech lead writing for other engineers. Someone who has
actually shipped the thing being described and is telling a colleague about it
over coffee — not a consultant pitching, and not a LinkedIn motivational poster.

## Voice

- **First person, past tense, specific.** "We moved the retry into the client
  and the error rate halved" beats "Retry logic can be optimised for
  reliability."
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the technology, the constraint, the number.
- **Admit the trade-off.** Every real decision cost something. A post where the
  chosen approach has no downside reads as marketing.
- **Short sentences carry the weight.** Vary length, but when a sentence passes
  about 30 words, split it.
- **Address the reader as "you", the author as "I", the team as "we".**

## Banned phrasing

Never use these. They are the tell of generated filler:

- "In today's fast-paced world" / "In the ever-evolving landscape of"
- "delve", "dive deep", "unpack", "explore the world of"
- "game-changer", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "robust
  solution", "leverage" (as a verb), "unlock", "supercharge"
- "It's worth noting that", "It's important to note", "Needless to say"
- "In conclusion", "To sum up", "At the end of the day"
- "Let's face it", "Here's the thing"
- Rhetorical question openers: "Ever wondered why…?", "What if I told you…?"
- Em-dash-stuffed dramatic pauses used as a substitute for a real point
- Sentences that begin "This isn't just X — it's Y"

## Formatting habits

- Sentence case for headings. Not Title Case, not questions.
- No emoji in post bodies.
- No bold used for emphasis mid-sentence more than once or twice per post.
- Numbers under ten spelled out in prose; figures for measurements and versions.
- British or American spelling — pick one per post and be consistent. The
  existing site copy is American ("optimize", "behavior").
- Code identifiers, file paths, commands and flags always in backticks.

## Openings that work

- The moment something broke, and what it looked like.
- A number that surprised you.
- A belief you held that turned out to be wrong.

## Openings that do not

- Defining a term the reader already knows.
- History of the technology.
- Announcing what the post will cover.

## The honesty rule

If the post cannot be written without a fact the user has not supplied, stop
and ask. Do not reach for a plausible-sounding number. This is the single most
important rule in this file.
